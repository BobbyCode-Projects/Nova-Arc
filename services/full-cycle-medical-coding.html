Clinical Revenue Risk

Why Sub-Par Coding Drains Clinical Cash Flow & Invites Audits

Inaccurate code assignment, missed secondary comorbidities, and unbundled surgical procedures drain millions in legitimate reimbursement while exposing organizations to severe payer audit sanctions.

Under-Coding & Uncaptured Comorbidities (8–15% Revenue Loss)

Overworked clinical staff and non-specialized coders routinely under-code complex encounters—defaulting to lower Evaluation and Management (E/M) levels (e.g. 99213 instead of 99214) or omitting crucial secondary CC/MCC diagnoses. This results in depressed MS-DRG weights and an unrecoverable 8%–15% cash loss.

Chronic Coder Shortages & Ballooning DNFB Backlogs

The national shortage of certified medical coders leads to unmanageable chart backlogs. Health systems and medical practices frequently experience 6 to 14 days of Discharged Not Final Billed (DNFB) delays, trapping millions in unbilled claims and paralyzing operational cash flow.

NCCI Edits Violations & RAC Recoupment Exposure

Improper modifier usage (-25, -59, -XS) or billing mutually exclusive code pairs triggers immediate clearinghouse rejection or commercial downcoding. Even worse, uncorroborated unbundling invites retrospective Medicare Recovery Audit Contractor (RAC) audits and devastating extrapolated clawbacks.

Correct Coding Architecture

CMS NCCI Edit Compliance Engine

How Nova Arc prevents unbundling rejections and RAC audit clawbacks through automated screening against CMS National Correct Coding Initiative rules.

Procedure-to-Procedure (PTP) & MUE Framework

CMS established the National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) to prevent improper payment when incorrect code combinations are submitted. Nova Arc's AI coding engine automatically compares all billed procedures against the quarterly CMS NCCI tables before claims reach the clearinghouse.

✓
PTP Column 1 / Column 2 Edits

Identifies code pairs that should not be reported together. Flags whether the edit allows an NCCI-associated modifier (Modifier Indicator "1") or strictly prohibits unbundling under any circumstance (Modifier Indicator "0").

✓
Medically Unlikely Edits (MUE) Validation

Screens the maximum units of service (UOS) a provider could report for a single beneficiary on a single date of service based on anatomical constraints or clinical common sense.

✓
Audited Modifier Protocol (-25, -59, and X{EPSU})

We enforce CMS distinct procedural rules: preferentially utilizing targeted CMS modifiers -XE (Separate Encounter), -XS (Separate Structure), -XP (Separate Practitioner), and -XU (Unusual Non-Overlapping Service) over generic -59 modifiers to avert automated payer prepayment audits.
