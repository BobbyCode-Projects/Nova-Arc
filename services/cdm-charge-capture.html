Clinical & Financial Disconnects

The Hidden Revenue Drain in Hospital-Based Physician Practices

Radiology, Pathology, and Anesthesia practices deliver services inside facility walls but bill on separate professional fee schedules. When clinical logs are disconnected from billing, massive charge leakage and audit exposure occur.

Silent Charge Leakage Across Facility Silos

Hospital-based specialists perform procedures recorded in hospital-owned systems (e.g. PACS imaging archives, pathology LIS specimen logs, operating room flowsheets). Without automated cross-system reconciliation, 7% to 14% of completed clinical procedures never generate a professional bill—including IV contrast, 3D post-processing, special pathology stains, and anesthesia nerve blocks.

Practice Impact: ~$780,000 to $2.1M in unbilled legitimate clinical services forfeited annually per 20-physician group.

Outdated Chargemaster & Arbitrary Fee Pricing

Many hospital-based CDM fee schedules have not undergone comprehensive competitive market analysis in over 3 years. Arbitrary price markups cause claims to fall below commercial payer allowed maximums on percent-of-charge contracts, leaving significant revenue on the table while exposing facilities to CMS Price Transparency fines.

Practice Impact: Sub-optimal fee schedule yields cost practices 5% to 9% on non-capitated commercial collections.

Split-Billing (TC/26) & Concurrency Penalties

Hospital-based specialists must navigate complex split-billing rules: separating professional interpretations (Modifier 26) from hospital technical components (TC), while managing strict anesthesia medical direction ratios (1:4 concurrency). Missing modifiers or time discrepancies trigger instant payer downcoding and catastrophic OIG repayment audits.

Practice Impact: Medical direction downcoded to medical supervision (AD) slashes anesthesia reimbursement by 50%.

CDM Pricing & Missed-Charge FAQs

Answers to common questions regarding Chargemaster pricing reviews, clinical log reconciliation, and hospital-based specialty split billing.

This is the standard operating model for private hospital-based groups. We establish secure automated interfaces (via encrypted SFTP, VPN, or read-only database connections) into the hospital's clinical systems—such as the PACS imaging archive, laboratory LIS, and perioperative flowsheets. Our software normalizes and ingests procedure logs daily, then matches them against your practice management billing database using patient MRN, date of service, accession number, and provider NPI. Any clinical event missing a corresponding billed charge is instantly routed to our specialty reconciliation team for review.

In Radiology, the most frequent missed charges are 3D volumetric rendering (CPT 76376/76377), IV contrast agents (HCPCS Q-codes), fluoroscopic guidance (77002), and computer-aided detection (CAD). In Pathology, charge leakage commonly affects special histochemical stains (88312/88313), immunohistochemistry first vs. additional stains (88342/88341), in-situ hybridization, and multiple tissue block levels within a single surgical container. In Anesthesia, practices frequently miss postoperative acute pain management nerve blocks (e.g. TAP blocks 64488 with modifier 59), ultrasound guidance for vascular access (76942), invasive line placements (arterial line 36620, central line 36556), and physical status modifiers (P3/P4/P5) that add legitimate base units.

Our CDM pricing audit utilizes a multi-layered financial model. First, we cross-reference your current fee schedule against your commercial payer contracts to ensure that no charge falls below a payer's allowable fee—preventing "lesser-of" contract underpayments. Second, we benchmark each code against local geographic Medicare RBRVS multiples (typically 250% to 375%) and regional 75th percentile market data. Third, we produce fully formatted CMS Machine-Readable Files (MRF in JSON/CSV) complying with federal price transparency rules, establishing a defensible, justifiable pricing structure that protects against regulatory scrutiny.

Under CMS TEFRA rules, an anesthesiologist medically directing CRNAs can oversee a maximum of 4 concurrent cases (1:4 ratio) and must personally perform 7 mandatory steps (pre-op evaluation, prescribing anesthesia plan, personally participating in induction/emergence, etc.). If concurrency exceeds 4 cases for even one minute, or if an emergency in one room pulls the physician away during induction in another, medical direction is invalid, and the claim drops to medical supervision (Modifier AD), slashing payment by 50%. Nova Arc’s reconciliation engine overlays exact AIMS OR minute-by-minute concurrency maps against billing entries, ensuring proper assignment of modifiers QK, QX, QY, or QZ while preventing audit clawbacks.
